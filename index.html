Asteroid Miner

Sea of Thieves, but in space.

Launching soon on Roblox Product lead · 4-person team Luau · Roblox Studio · Rojo · Git Summer 2026 – present

The product story

Crew a ship, mine asteroids, get your haul home before space pirates take it, and reinvest in your base. I lead the team, and I own the home base, the tycoon economy and the fuel systems. The hardest part wasn't building features. It was deciding which ones to keep.

Finding the spine

By mid-summer the sandbox build had about 20 systems and no real decision in the loop. A simpler build had a 10-slot hold and ore that's worth more the farther you fly. Together those force a "push further or bank it" choice every run. I called it: this is a mining-run game, and the tycoon is the floor that funds runs, not the point.

Cutting with evidence

An economy audit found that colonies cost 750 credits and earned ~4,750/hr, while an Iron Extractor cost 2,000 and settled near 3,240/hr. Idle income beat actually playing. We cut colonies, the space bike and the asteroid anchor, shrank the generator ladder from 8 tiers to 4, and capped passive income clearly below what runs pay.

Fair across progression

The old pirate-raid penalty was a flat 3,000 credits: 100% of a mid-game player's net worth, and 3% of a late-game player's. I replaced it with cargo-only loss, so you can lose the run but never the bank. Running dry far from home works the same way: a free emergency recall that costs your cargo.

Engineering

  • Server-authoritative fuel. Both free and paid refueling run entirely on the server, which closed a client-side spoofing exploit.
  • Four docking bugs, verified live. A velocity-reset trap, floor clipping on recall, over-anchoring 78 ship parts, and a network-ownership race on the seat.
  • Shipped, then cut, my own UI. I replaced 84 per-slot interaction prompts with one kiosk after seeing the clutter in play.
  • AI-native team workflow. Each teammate runs a Claude Code session against a shared repo synced to Studio via Rojo. Sessions write daily update files, and a bot posts them to our standup channel.
  • Playtest-driven. We ran competitive playtests of comparable Roblox tycoon games to set priorities, and I keep a written log of every call and why we made it.

Vivarium

Nothing is scripted. Population cycles, predator–prey swings and extinctions all emerge.

Live, plays in the browser Solo JavaScript · Three.js · Node · Neuroevolution Sept – Oct 2026

Two modes, one engine

Vivarium is an open-ended Amazon ecosystem with eight species. Every animal is an individual with a heritable four-trait genome (mass, speed, sense, metabolism), and you can watch selection shift those traits over generations.

Arena drops sixteen named animals into a shrinking ring. Before each match, it simulates 200 matches of that exact roster to give you the odds. You pick a winner, and afterwards a "Play of the Game" replays the most surprising moment. The fighters are cartoon animals with faces and signature moves, and the Arena now has touch controls and a phone layout.

  • Real ecology, not magic numbers. Metabolism follows Kleiber's law (mass0.75), grazing uses a Holling type II response, carcasses feed the soil, and territory pressure sets local carrying capacity.
  • Deterministic engine. It never calls Math.random, so a seed plus a roster always replays the same match. That one property is what makes the odds, the replays and headless balance testing in Node possible.
  • Balancing as an optimization problem. Fixed-step tuning oscillated, because a 10% edge roughly triples a species' win rate. I switched to a stochastic-approximation tuner and added a hidden "form" roll, which finally converged.
  • Damping a boom-bust. Ocelots and harpy eagles hunted even when full, overshot, and wiped out the agoutis and howlers about every 90 seconds. Now pursuit predators only hunt below 65% energy, and every species survives a headless 480-second run.

Evolved brains

Any animal can trade its hand-tuned instincts for a small neural network. A herbivore senses 16 things: where the nearest threat, food and kin are and how close, plus crowding, its own energy, age, whether it can breed, and two memory values. These feed 8 tanh hidden units, which drive 6 outputs: turn, throttle, head down to eat, breed, and the two memory values, which loop back as inputs. Predators get their own wiring with 19 inputs (their target and a backup prey, each with catch odds and size, rival predators, strike range) and decide when to lunge and when to switch prey.

The weights are the genome. They mutate at birth and selection does the rest. There's no gradient descent: a brain survives only if the animal carrying it eats, escapes and breeds.

A fair A/B test

Mixed mode runs instinct and brain lineages side by side in one world, and each lineage has its own immigration floor, so a crash can't hand the other side a free win. Brains start wired to copy instinct plus noise, so the comparison starts at parity. After 25 generations of evolution, evolved agouti brains took 86% of the agouti population when dropped back in against instinct. They bred at lower energy than instinct does. Across the other herbivores, brains won for howlers and tapirs, and capybaras still did better on instinct.

Brains that over-hunt

Predator brains learned to out-hunt instinct and wiped out the small prey within about 200 seconds. Rather than add a rule, I made hunting cost more for both lineages: a lunge burns 10× normal energy and a miss means a 15-second rest. Over-hunters starve, so restraint gets selected. I swept 11 settings on a seeded test harness. At the chosen one, 4 of 5 seeds keep every prey species alive while brain predators hold 49–89% of the predators. Settings strong enough to pass the fifth starved the predators down to the immigration floor, so I took the 4 of 5.

Instinct stays the control

All of this is gated off in pure Instinct mode. A seeded Instinct run produces the same state hash before and after every brain change, so the original ecology is still there to compare against.

Music tools

Live · single HTML file · Web Audio

Rhythm Reader

A rhythm-reading trainer styled like an engraved method book. Drag notes into bars, or type them in shorthand (q q [e e e] h.), then play them back. A red "pencil" follows each note with the count written underneath.

It covers simple and compound metres, triplets, ties, dotted values and rests. It has five instrument voices, four metronome clicks, a count-in, subdivision and repeat.

Fret Driller

Drills for learning the guitar fretboard. Find the note and Name the note quiz you on positions. Sequences has you play a melody in order, with an optional strict mode that checks the octave and keeps you in one position. Scale patterns covers 12 scales and modes in every key, played up, down, in thirds or in groups.

Explore maps any scale or arpeggio across all 24 frets and can overlay a second scale for comparison. It tracks accuracy and speed for every fret and shows them as a heatmap. The note drills bring up your weak spots more often.

It now has a bass mode: a 4-string neck with its own stats and best times. The menu is split into Learn, which opens on the fretboard map, and Drill. Ranks come from lifetime correct answers on each instrument, and the whole app has a Jedi/Sith theme.

The newest page is Jam: a synthesized backing band (drums, bass and keys, five grooves) loops a chord progression you can edit, while the fretboard lights up the current chord's tones by degree and previews the next chord. A chord parser handles common jazz and pop symbols with correct spelling.

NFL Predictor

Win probabilities for every game, built only on what's known before kickoff.

Live, new picks each week Python · PyTorch · scikit-learn · pandas Oct 2026

The model, and the benchmark it doesn't beat

A five-model ensemble (logistic regression, ridge margin, gradient boosting and two neural networks) gives each game a win probability. Every feature uses only data from before kickoff. Vegas lines are a benchmark, never an input.

On 1,218 held-out games (2022 through 2026 week 5) it picked 66.3% of winners. That's 2.4 points better than Elo and 11 better than always picking the home team, but about a point behind Vegas, which it beat only in 2024 (71.9% vs. 70.5%).

  • Lean Vegas when they disagree. In the 170 held-out games where the two picked different winners, Vegas was right 53.5% of the time and the model 45.9%. So the model's job is to rank confidence and flag games, not to overrule the market.
  • Pregame roster ratings. Each player is rated by recency-weighted snap share, with a draft-pick prior for rookies, then summed over the active roster before kickoff. That's how injuries and roster turnover reach the model. Starting-QB features carried the most unique value.
  • Overfitting was the main problem. With about 250 features and only 2,000–3,000 training games, the first networks overfit within about five epochs. An L2 penalty and input dropout stabilized them, and after that the MLP tied logistic regression: the signal is mostly linear.
  • Runs itself every week. Scheduled jobs pull fresh nflverse data, rebuild the roster ratings, retrain all five models and publish new picks on Sunday, Tuesday and Thursday mornings, plus a Friday-evening run after the final injury reports. Each prediction freezes at kickoff, and the model only trains on weeks before the one it's predicting, so the live record can't be quietly revised.
  • Me vs. the model vs. Vegas. I enter my own probability for every game on a private pick sheet. After kickoff, the public page grades my picks next to the model's and Vegas's, so I find out whether my gut adds anything.
  • Scored once. Models were tuned on 2018–21 and evaluated a single time on 2022–26. Dev and held-out log-loss matched (0.623 vs. 0.624), so model selection didn't overfit.
