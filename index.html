01

Asteroid Miner

Sea of Thieves, but in space.

Launching soon on Roblox Product lead · 4-person team Luau · Roblox Studio · Rojo · Git Summer 2026 – present

The product story

Crew a ship, mine asteroids, get your haul home before space pirates take it, and reinvest in your base. I lead the team, and I own the home base, the tycoon economy and the fuel systems. The hardest part wasn't building features. It was deciding which ones to keep.

Finding the spine

By mid-summer the sandbox build had about 20 systems and no real decision in the loop. A simpler build had a 10-slot hold and ore that's worth more the farther you fly. Together those force a "push further or bank it" choice every run. I called it: this is a mining-run game, and the tycoon is the floor that funds runs, not the point.

Cutting with evidence

An economy audit found that colonies cost 750 credits and earned ~4,750/hr, while an Iron Extractor cost 2,000 and settled near 3,240/hr. Idle income beat actually playing. We cut colonies, the space bike and the asteroid anchor, shrank the generator ladder from 8 tiers to 4, and capped passive income clearly below what runs pay.

Fair across progression

The old pirate-raid penalty was a flat 3,000 credits: 100% of a mid-game player's net worth, and 3% of a late-game player's. I replaced it with cargo-only loss, so you can lose the run but never the bank. Running dry far from home works the same way: a free emergency recall that costs your cargo.

Engineering

  • Server-authoritative fuel. Both free and paid refueling run entirely on the server, which closed a client-side spoofing exploit.
  • Four docking bugs, verified live. A velocity-reset trap, floor clipping on recall, over-anchoring 78 ship parts, and a network-ownership race on the seat.
  • Shipped, then cut, my own UI. I replaced 84 per-slot interaction prompts with one kiosk after seeing the clutter in play.
  • AI-native team workflow. Each teammate runs a Claude Code session against a shared repo synced to Studio via Rojo. Sessions write daily update files, and a bot posts them to our standup channel.
  • Playtest-driven. We ran competitive playtests of comparable Roblox tycoon games to set priorities, and I keep a written log of every call and why we made it.

Vivarium

Nothing is scripted. Population cycles, predator–prey swings and extinctions all emerge.

Live, plays in the browser Solo JavaScript · Three.js · Node Sept 2026

Two modes, one engine

Vivarium is an open-ended Amazon ecosystem with eight species. Every animal is an individual with a heritable four-trait genome (mass, speed, sense, metabolism), and you can watch selection shift those traits over generations.

Arena drops sixteen named animals into a shrinking ring. Before each match, it simulates 200 matches of that exact roster to give you the odds. You pick a winner, and afterwards a "Play of the Game" replays the most surprising moment.

  • Real ecology, not magic numbers. Metabolism follows Kleiber's law (mass0.75), grazing uses a Holling type II response, carcasses feed the soil, and territory pressure sets local carrying capacity.
  • Deterministic engine. It never calls Math.random, so a seed plus a roster always replays the same match. That one property is what makes the odds, the replays and headless balance testing in Node possible.
  • Balancing as an optimization problem. Fixed-step tuning oscillated, because a 10% edge roughly triples a species' win rate. I switched to a stochastic-approximation tuner and added a hidden "form" roll, which finally converged.

By the numbers

~1,900animals simulated at a 10 ms frame (spatial hash, instanced meshes)
200simulated matches behind every pre-match odds board
13arena species, each with its own move (death roll, constrict, howl)
0.75–1.25×each species' share of wins vs. a fair share in balanced Pairs

Rhythm Reader

A rhythm-reading trainer styled like an engraved method book. Drag notes into bars, or type them in shorthand (q q [e e e] h.), then play them back. A red "pencil" follows each note with the count written underneath.

It covers simple and compound metres, triplets, ties, dotted values and rests. It has five instrument voices, four metronome clicks, a count-in, subdivision and repeat.
